Delivered UL throughput under two different RIC control actions, each applied to the same DRB and observed for 45 s, aligned on a common "time since cap applied" axis (shaded region). DRB QoS MFBR (Style 1/Action 1, blue) is configured for a 2 Mbps rate target and converges to a steady-state mean of 1.77 Mbps (std 0.01 Mbps, t > 5 s). PRB quota (Style 2/Action 6, green) caps this UE to 10% of the cell's PRBs (~11 of 106 PRBs for this cell's 20 MHz/15 kHz-SCS configuration) and happens to settle at a steady-state mean of 2.23 Mbps (std 0.03 Mbps) under the channel conditions and MCS in effect during this run - a number the operator could not have specified directly, since the control action only accepts a PRB percentage, not a bitrate. The two steady-state rates happen to be numerically close here, but only one of them (MFBR) is the value that was actually requested; the PRB-quota outcome is a downstream side effect of the current radio conditions and would shift if the UE's achievable MCS changed, without any change to the 10% configuration itself. A second, mechanistic difference is also visible: MFBR shows a brief near-zero transient in the first ~2 s (its token-bucket rate limiter draining the pre-existing uncapped backlog, see Plot 1's README), while the PRB quota - a hard resource ceiling enforced from the next scheduling opportunity, with no equivalent token bucket - transitions directly to its steady level.
